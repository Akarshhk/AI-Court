Case: People of the State of California v. Orenthal James Simpson Charge: Two counts of murder The defense argued that the prosecution’s case was compromised by police misconduct, evidence mishandling, contamination, and possible planting of evidence. The defense focused heavily on Detective Mark Fuhrman’s credibility and alleged racism, and argued that once the jury could not trust the messenger, it could not trust the physical evidence. Main defense points
The defense claimed evidence was mishandled and possibly contaminated. The defense argued some evidence may have been planted by police, especially Fuhrman-related evidence. The glove evidence became a major point because the glove appeared not to fit Simpson in court. The defense argued that police credibility was damaged by Fuhrman’s racist statements and Fifth Amendment invocation. The defense maintained that the prosecution had not eliminated reasonable doubt. Defense conclusion
The defense asked jurors to acquit because the chain of custody, police credibility, and handling of forensic evidence created reasonable doubt.
Case: People of the State of California v. Orenthal James Simpson Charge: Two counts of murder
The prosecution’s theory was that O.J. Simpson had a history of domestic violence, giving him motive, and that he killed Nicole Brown Simpson and Ronald Goldman during the late evening of June 12, 1994. Prosecutors emphasized that blood evidence linked Simpson to the crime scene, his vehicle, and his home, and they also relied on witness timing evidence, including Allan Park’s testimony and the timeline placing Simpson near the scene during the critical window. Main prosecution points
Simpson had a documented history of domestic violence toward Nicole Brown Simpson, which prosecutors used to establish motive. The physical evidence included blood at the crime scene, in Simpson’s Bronco, and in Simpson’s home that prosecutors said matched Simpson and the victims. A bloody glove at the scene and a matching glove found at Simpson’s property were treated as key evidence. Simpson’s fresh cut on his hand was argued to be consistent with the attack.
Witness timing evidence suggested Simpson was at or near the residence during the murder window. Prosecution conclusion The state asked the jury to find that the blood trail, glove evidence, shoe evidence, and timeline proved Simpson’s guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
